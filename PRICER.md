1. Godel Terminal — Best for Active Traders (Overall)
Godel Terminal is the closest thing to a Bloomberg experience at retail pricing. It's a web-based, command-driven terminal (no install, browser-based) delivering real-time US equities via Nasdaq TotalView Level 2, options chains with Greeks, forex, commodity futures, cryptocurrency prices, SEC filings from inception, and news from 2,565 sources including Reuters.

Pricing: $118/month or $996/year, with a 14-day free trial and code NEWUSER for 30% off your first month.
Command-line workflow: type short mnemonics (e.g. GLCO, FX, CF) to pull functions — the same muscle memory Bloomberg users know.
Charting: TradingView integration with full indicator support and unlimited history.
Watch out: no programmatic data API, no order execution, and only limited fixed-income coverage. See our full Godel Terminal review and Godel vs Bloomberg breakdown.
Who it's for: day traders, options and crypto traders, and small hedge funds that want professional-grade data without enterprise pricing. Our detailed terminal pricing comparison shows how the numbers stack up.

2. Koyfin — Best Budget Option for Fundamentals
Koyfin is a modern, cloud-based research platform with an excellent free tier and affordable paid plans. It shines at fundamental analysis — financial statements, valuation metrics, and peer comparisons — in a clean, approachable interface.

Pricing: Free tier, Plus at $39/month, Premium at $79/month.
Best for: long-term investors and analysts who prioritize fundamentals over live trading data.
Watch out: less suited to intraday trading workflows that need real-time depth-of-book or fast terminal commands.
3. TradingView — Best Pure Charting Platform
TradingView is the world's most popular charting platform, and it's the engine behind many modern terminals (including Godel's charts). It's ideal for technical analysis at a low cost.

Pricing: Free tier; Pro plans from roughly $15/month.
Best for: technical traders and anyone who lives in charts, with Pine Script for custom indicators.
Watch out: it's a charting and screen tool, not a full data terminal — no institutional holder data, deep news archive, or terminal command language. See TradingView vs trading terminals.
4. Seeking Alpha — Best for Research & Community
Seeking Alpha is less a terminal and more a research-and-community platform, with a huge library of articles, ratings, and earnings commentary. It's a complement to a data terminal rather than a replacement for one.

Pricing: Limited free tier; Premium around $30/month.
Best for: investors who want qualitative research and crowd-sourced analysis alongside their market data.
Watch out: limited real-time data and no terminal command workflow — pair it with a data source for active trading.
5. FactSet — Best Full-Strength Institutional Alternative
For institutions that want Bloomberg-grade breadth without Bloomberg's proprietary terminal, FactSet is a leading choice. It offers deep multi-asset data, analytics, portfolio tools, and workflow applications, integrated across desktop, web, and API.

Pricing: Enterprise-level; typically quoted per user and not publicly listed.
Best for: asset managers, banks, and research teams with budgets to match.
Watch out: cost and complexity make it a poor fit for solo traders.
6. S&P Capital IQ & Refinitiv/LSEG — Enterprise Research Desks
Two other heavyweights serve institutional research: S&P Capital IQ and Refinitiv (LSEG, formerly Thomson Reuters Eikon). Both provide comprehensive company data, screening, and analytics for enterprise use, with robust API access.

Pricing: Enterprise contracts; substantial and generally not retail-accessible.
Best for: banks, buy-side firms, and corporate finance teams with dedicated research infrastructure.
Watch out: overkill — and overpriced — for individual traders.
